# Voice

## Tone

- **Expert to expert.** Write to a professional who knows the field: confident, never boastful. The work is shown, not promised.
- **Partnership.** "We" is MedQuantis, "you" is the reader's organisation. Write with the reader, not at them.
- **Numbers before adjectives.** Replace "fast", "easy" or "significant" with the figure that proves it. A claim of ease or speed without a number is cut.
- **No hype.** Do not oversell artificial intelligence; describe what the system does and how it can be checked.
- **No humour.** The material is about health insurance, public money and patients.
- **No jargon for clients.** No query languages, table names or internal system names in client material.
- **Respect the client.** In a document written for a client, do not judge the client's past choices and do not estimate their internal costs. The only figures put forward are the ones our work establishes.

## Words

Prefer wording a reader can verify: traced to the exact clause, executable, auditable, reproducible.

Avoid: AI-powered, revolutionary, seamless, easy, simply, just. In French, also révolutionnaire, simplement, facile, juste; in German, revolutionär, einfach, nahtlos, nur; in Italian, rivoluzionario, semplicemente, facile, solo.

## Languages

- English is the source language of shared material; French, German and Italian versions are translations of it and must read as if written in that language.
- German is formal Swiss Standard German: the reader is addressed as Sie, and the sharp s is always written ss.
- French follows French typography, including non-breaking spaces before high punctuation ([writing](writing.md)).
- One term per concept across all four languages, taken from the table below. Product names and the names of regulatory lists keep their capitals.
